What Does Failing Window Sealant Look Like?

Visual signs of seal failure and what each one means for your home.

The sealant and weatherstripping around a window are the first line of defense against Fort Myers’ humidity, wind-driven rain, and heat. When they fail, the damage is often happening somewhere you can’t see until it’s significant. Here’s what to look for.

Cracked, shrunken, or gapped caulk lines

Fresh caulk is smooth and flexible. Failing caulk cracks, pulls away from the frame, or shrinks enough to leave visible gaps. In Florida’s heat and UV exposure, exterior caulk typically shows wear years before an interior-only home would.

Discoloration or chalking around the frame edges

A dusty, faded, or discolored strip around the perimeter of a window often means the sealant has broken down chemically, not just physically. It's no longer flexible enough to move with the frame through daily heat cycles.

Water stains on the interior sill or nearby wall

This is the sign to take most seriously. If water is making it past the seal and staining the interior sill, drywall, or trim, moisture has likely been getting into the wall cavity for a while already — which raises the risk of hidden rot or mold well before it’s visible on the surface.

Mold or mildew smell near the window

A musty smell concentrated near one window, even without visible staining, often means moisture is accumulating somewhere inside the wall assembly around that opening.

A draft that wasn’t there before

Sealant and weatherstripping wear gradually, so a draft that’s new or worsening is a reliable sign the seal around that window is failing, even if you can’t see obvious cracking yet.

Why resealing usually isn’t the long-term fix

Surface caulking can mask early sealant wear for a little while, but it doesn’t address what’s usually the underlying cause: an aging frame that has moved, warped, or corroded enough that it no longer holds a seal the way it did when it was installed. On a window old enough to be showing these signs, a fresh bead of caulk is often just delaying a decision rather than solving the problem — especially once water staining is already visible. At that point, replacing the window addresses the actual cause instead of the symptom.

What to do if you're seeing these signs

A free on-site evaluation can tell you quickly whether what you’re seeing is early-stage wear worth monitoring, or a sign that the window system itself is due for replacement.
